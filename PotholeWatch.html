The problem

Potholes wreck vehicles and cause crashes, especially for two-wheelers. Most roads get fixed only after enough people complain, and the complaints arrive as unstructured calls, photos and tweets with no way to tell a dangerous crater from a cosmetic crack. Surveys are slow and expensive, so repair crews often work in the wrong order.

The solution

PotholeWatch is a three-step pipeline that runs entirely in the browser:

Detect. The phone's accelerometer is compared to a running baseline. A jolt above the threshold, debounced to one per 1.5 s, is tagged with the current GPS fix. Jolt strength gives a rough depth estimate.

Report. Citizens add depth, width, road type and traffic, plus an optional photo that is shrunk on-device.

Triage. Each pothole gets a 0–100 score: (0.6·depth/10 + 0.4·width/80) × road factor × traffic factor, then extra points for duplicate reports. Tiers: Critical ≥70 (fix within 24 h), High ≥45 (7 days), Medium ≥25 (30 days), Low (monitor). The list is sorted so a crew can work top-down.

Honest limitations

Thresholds and weights are hand-tuned guesses, not calibrated on real road data. Speed bumps, rail crossings and rough phone mounting cause false positives. Data lives only in this browser (localStorage), so there is no shared backend or authority hand-off yet.

Next steps

Speed-normalise jolts and filter known bumps, add a shared database with municipal ticketing, and train a small vision model on photos to verify size automatically.
